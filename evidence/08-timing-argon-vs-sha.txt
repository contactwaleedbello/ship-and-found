--- The Inversion (In Plain Words) ---
You hash once per login; an attacker hashes billions of times in a brute-force attack. Spending 50 milliseconds to verify a customer during login costs the human user nothing measurable, but multiplying the attacker's cracking equation by 50 milliseconds across billions of attempts turns an attack that would succeed in seconds into one that takes decades. Fast algorithms are designed for file integrity; slow, memory-hard algorithms are mandatory for passwords.

--- Salting Mechanics ---
• What Salting Does: A salt is cryptographically random data prepended or appended to the password prior to hashing. It ensures that two users with the exact same password ("password123") produce completely different hash outputs.
• Does Argon2id Handle it?: Yes. Argon2id generates and embeds a 16-byte random salt directly into the serialized hash string ($argon2id$v=19$m=65536,t=3,p=4$<salt>$<hash>).
• Attacker Consequence if Skipped: Without salts, an attacker who steals the database can compute a single precomputed rainbow table of common passwords and crack every user sharing the same password simultaneously in $O(1)$ time.
